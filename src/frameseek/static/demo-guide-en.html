FrameSeek is a desktop film room for investigating basketball footage. Questions, cited source frames and follow-ups stay together in a conversation and a spatial canvas.

About this demo: the parcel video is synthetic and consists of three static scenes. Its answers are preset; this demonstration does not call a model or establish live video-recognition quality.

Try the parcel demo

  1. Open the workspace above on a desktop browser. Select the synthetic parcel video if another source is open; this does not grant access to another person's uploaded video.
  2. In the question box, enter When is the parcel in the center? and send it. This exact English phrase is supported by this preset demo.
  3. The preset answer says the parcel is in the center from 00:05 to 00:11. Select a frame labelled Replay to seek to that moment in the player.
  4. Select Ask a follow-up under the answer. Enter What happens next? and send it. The next preset answer shows the right-side stage beginning at 00:11; its 00:13 frame replays at that source time.
  5. Use 对话 to see the conversation or 画布 to see the same investigation on the canvas. Refresh to check that the two questions and their evidence remain saved.

Sports and NVIDIA/Nebius evidence

A previous limited basketball test used NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Super VL through Nebius Token Factory and returned scoreboard evidence at source time 05:26.1 with a contextual follow-up. Those saved results were partial. They do not prove that a fresh judge session can run new sports analysis.

The newer reusable basketball index was tested separately with a local Qwen model. It is not a Nemotron result. The sports imagery on the landing page is AI-created illustration. Private basketball uploads are not shared by this guide.

Uploaded-video analysis requires a configured processor and its existing authorization. This guide does not enable a model, grant a budget, or submit a question automatically.

What the demo does not prove

The preset answer is not a new model response. A sampled video index can miss brief appearances, and a cited frame is not proof of a complete event count or a person’s identity. The live NVIDIA/Nebius path and fresh-user real-video experience require separate verification.
